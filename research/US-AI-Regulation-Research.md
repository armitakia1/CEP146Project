
# The US Approach to AI Regulation

## Research Questions

1. What laws and policies currently regulate artificial intelligence in the United States?
2. How has the Trump administration approached AI regulation since January 2025?
3. What are the potential benefits and risks of reducing AI regulations?

## Background

The United States does not have established federal laws specifically regulating artificial intelligence, unlike the European Union's AI Act. However, there have been advances under different presidential administrations. The United States has federal laws and state regulations that address certain AI-related risks (Congressional Research Service, 2025; Davtyan, 2025).

US AI policies seem to rely on voluntary commitments; various states have proposed and continue to enact AI laws. Although there have been advances in how AI should be used, it seems that the US acts cautiously regarding how the private sector might be affected, while maintaining a stable economy. In fact, this approach aids competitiveness in the AI economy (Davtyan, 2025). This might influence how the country views and filters AI regulations.

In the United States, there are no comprehensive regulations for AI technologies. Congress evaluates and reports on requirements, although it does not directly create regulations on the use of technologies (Harris, 2025).

In 2016, the Obama administration laid the foundation for federal AI policies addressing ethical challenges and national security, including the creation of the "AI RMF" (AI Risk Management Framework), anticipating and preparing for the development of artificial intelligence (Davtyan, 2025).

During President Trump's first administration, the United States established the "American AI Initiative," which implemented guidance on AI use while promoting a balance between innovation, civil liberties, privacy, and national security (Davtyan, 2025).

In 2022, under the Biden administration, five core principles for using AI responsibly were established to prevent civil and human rights abuses (Davtyan, 2025).

## Current US Government Policies

In January 2025, the White House issued an executive order prioritizing American leadership in AI and reducing barriers to its development.

In July 2025, the administration released *America's AI Action Plan*, emphasizing innovation, economic competitiveness, and fewer regulatory obstacles (The White House, 2025a, 2025b).

In December 2025, the administration called for a national AI policy framework and directed federal officials to challenge certain state AI regulations (The White House, 2025c).

## Arguments For and Against Regulation

**Arguments for fewer restrictions:** The United States focuses on how the economy and the private sector might be affected by strict AI regulations and instead proposes guidelines for the safe use of AI. It believes that AI can help companies reduce costs and increase innovation, allowing US companies to stand out internationally and benefit the country's financial system. As Harris (2025) states, "stakeholders have raised concerns that potential constraints to U.S. AI innovation from new regulations might lead to the United States losing its historically dominant position in a global 'race' to develop advanced AI."

**Arguments for stronger safeguards:** Advances in AI can come with risks, such as cybersecurity threats, privacy breaches, and accountability issues. Without established regulations, the harm might increase unnoticed and eventually spiral out of control (Harris, 2025).
