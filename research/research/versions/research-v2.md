## Moffatt v. Air Canada (Canada, 2024)

A British Columbia tribunal decided this in February 2024. Jake Moffatt asked Air Canada's website chatbot about bereavement fares after a family member died. The chatbot said he could claim the discount after the flight, which wasn't the airline's real policy. Air Canada argued the chatbot was a separate entity responsible for itself. The tribunal said no: the chatbot is part of the website, and the company answers for what's on it. The legal test was negligent misrepresentation. The award was small, about $812 including interest and fees (unchecked). It's a small claims tribunal, so I shouldn't overstate its weight as precedent.

## Benavides v. Tesla (United States)

A 2019 crash in Key Largo, Florida. The driver was using Autopilot, looked away for a dropped phone and hit a parked SUV. One person died and another was badly hurt. In August 2025 a federal jury in Miami found Tesla 33% responsible and the driver 67%. Tesla's share came to roughly $243 million, including $200 million in punitive damages. Tesla argued the driver alone was to blame and has appealed. The verdict isn't final.

## Uber self-driving crash (United States, 2018)

An Uber test vehicle in autonomous mode killed a pedestrian, Elaine Herzberg, in Tempe, Arizona. Prosecutors said Uber wasn't criminally liable (unchecked). The backup driver was charged with negligent homicide and later pleaded guilty to a lesser charge (unchecked). The NTSB blamed her inattention, but also criticized Uber's safety practices. I've seen mention that Uber settled with the family (unchecked).

## Mobley v. Workday (United States, AI hiring)

The plaintiff says Workday's AI screening tools discriminated by race, age and disability. Workday sells software and isn't the employer, so the plaintiff argues it's liable as the employers' agent. The court let the claims go ahead in 2024 and conditionally certified an age-discrimination group in 2025. These are allegations, not findings. I don't know the current status.

## Garcia v. Character Technologies (United States)

A wrongful-death suit after a 14-year-old's suicide following long conversations with a chatbot. In May 2025 a judge refused to dismiss on free speech grounds, saying she wasn't prepared to treat the chatbot's output as speech. Reports say the case settled in January 2026 (unchecked). This is a sensitive topic and I may pick a different example for the video.

## EU

The new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 treats software and AI as products and keeps no-fault liability. It applies to products placed on the market after December 9, 2026. The separate AI Liability Directive, proposed in 2022, was dropped in 2025 (details unchecked).

## Canada

The proposed Artificial Intelligence and Data Act was part of Bill C-27 and didn't pass. So there's no AI-specific federal liability law, and harms go through existing law. I need an official source on current plans.

## How my guesses held up

1. Courts treating AI as a person: no case found where one did. *Moffatt* rejected the idea.
2. The company answering for its chatbot: supported by *Moffatt*.
3. Shared blame: supported by *Benavides* and the Uber case.
4. No Canadian AI law: supported, but I need an official source.
5. The EU being ahead: partly. It dropped its AI liability proposal, and the product liability update does most of the work.
