## Topic assigned: 

Who is legally responsible for the actions of AI when AI does things that are completely unintended, and how are the consequences meant to be handled?

## Breaking the question into parts

1. Can an AI itself be legally responsible? This sets up the problem: if not the AI, then who?
2. Which humans or companies could be responsible: the developer, a vendor, the company using the tool, or the end user?
3. Which legal ideas are courts actually using, such as negligence, product liability, agency or criminal law?
4. Which real cases show this happening? Concrete examples will help a three-minute video.
5. What laws exist or are coming in Canada, the EU and the US?
6. How are consequences handled: damages, settlements, fines, criminal charges, changes to the product?
7. What is still unresolved? A good ending needs an open question.

## Assumptions

These are guesses to test, not facts.

1. Courts will not treat the AI as a person, so responsibility lands on a human or a company.
2. A company that deploys an AI tool is probably responsible for what it tells customers.
3. Self-driving and driver-assist cases probably split the blame between the company and the human.
4. Canada probably has no dedicated AI law yet.
5. The EU is probably further ahead on written rules.
