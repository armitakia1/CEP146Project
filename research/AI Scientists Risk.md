As AI has begun to make recent advancements and have made very big breakthroughs,
there has been a rising concern as people try to make AI 
act as AI scientists, IE: getting AI to try and replace the position of scientists when it comes to chemistry and biology
Because AI has been shown to be very good at analysis of data, people assume that AI scientists would be a good basis for an idea
However, because AI does not have the human capacity to analyse a situation (VS analysis of data),
there is a potential risk that AI could make severe errors due to an inability to recognise pathogens and chemical makeups and how they potentially react with someone's biology

the EU has attempted to establish EU-wide AI regulations, which at the moment is currently lacking
Because of the fact that an AI's ability to understand risk and analysis of a situation is limited,
AI's capabilities are believed to be too lacking to be able to handle complex tasks such as these
There is a lot of concerns currently over the fact that even though AI can look at data tables, 
AI cannot necessarily understand the context behind the data nor understand how to properly manipulate extremely complex biochemistry

Currently, the idea of having AI scientists poses a risk because there are no real proper safeguarding
especially because AI currently requires human supervision to be able to do what it is expected of AI to do when it comes to being an AI scientist, like what people want.

Because of the fact that AI is also capable of hallucinating and misinformation, any mistakes AI makes is extremely detrimental and dangerous to the idea of using agents as AI scientists currently, and is one of the potential risks of letting AI work on its own witohut supervision.
